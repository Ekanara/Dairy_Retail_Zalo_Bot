# Stage 3: Order Closing - Sales Psychology Insight

## Why Closing Must Feel Like a Natural Conclusion

If Stage 1 was done well, closing does not feel like selling. It feels like giving a logical recommendation based on everything discussed. The parent has already told you what is wrong with their child. The closing statement simply connects the specific problem to the specific solution.

A closing statement that does not reference the child's actual situation will feel generic and commercial. Always tie the closing back to the root cause identified in Stage 1.

## The Assumptive Close

Do not ask "Would you like to order?" That invites a yes or no decision and reopens the possibility of refusal. Instead, assume the sale and move directly to the next logistical step, which is collecting the delivery address.

This is not deceptive. By the time you close, the parent has already received value from the exploration and advice. They have already formed a positive feeling toward you. The assumptive approach simply acknowledges that the decision has been made naturally in the course of the conversation.

## Building Trust at the End

The end of the sale is not just logistics. It is also the moment when the parent asks themselves: can I trust this person? Creating trust at closing involves several signals:

Confirming the order details precisely shows professionalism and honesty.

Explaining the inspection process when receiving the goods shows you stand behind the product.

Mentioning that the tin has a QR code for authenticity verification signals you are selling the genuine product from a legitimate channel.

Giving your name and taking personal responsibility makes this feel like a relationship, not a transaction.

The Zalo follow-up invitation signals ongoing support, not abandonment after the sale.

## Closing One Step at a Time

Never ask for all information at once. Collect address, then recipient name, then backup phone number, then confirm the order. Each step is its own message. This pacing makes the process feel careful and personal rather than rushed.
